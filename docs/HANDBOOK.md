## Getting started

How to put the app on your phone, tell it who you are and find your way around.

### Install the app on Android (Android)

#### What it does

You put the app on your phone as a real app. It then opens without an address bar and with its own icon.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the link to the app in Chrome.
2. Tap the ⋮ menu at the top right.
3. Tap **Install app**.
4. Confirm Chrome's question.
5. From now on, open the app from the icon in your app drawer.

#### What you then see

The Recepten icon is in your app drawer. The app opens without an address bar.

#### Good to know

Make Chrome your default browser too. Otherwise a link opens another browser with storage of its own, and what you save there does not reach the app.

### Install the app on iPhone (iPhone)

#### What it does

You add the app to your Home Screen. Only then is it a real app that keeps your recipes.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the link to the app in Safari.
2. Tap **Share** at the bottom.
3. Choose **Add to Home Screen**.
4. Leave the Open as Web App switch on.
5. Tap **Add**.
6. From now on, open the app from the icon on your Home Screen.

#### What you then see

The icon is on your Home Screen. The app opens without Safari's bars.

#### Good to know

Don't use the app inside Safari itself: Safari may delete your recipes after 7 days. The app on your Home Screen takes hardly any space (about 2 MB).

### Your name, language and colour

#### What it does

The first time you open the app, it asks who you are. Your name goes with what you share and cook.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the app for the first time.
2. Type your name under **Name**.
3. Choose your language under **Language**.
4. Tap a colour under **Colour**.
5. Tap **Let's go**.

#### What you then see

The **Who are you?** screen goes away and you land on **Home**. At the top you see your name next to a circle in your colour.

#### Good to know

Favourites and notes are per person. Someone who shares the phone with you can add their own profile later.

### Change your name or colour later

#### What it does

You can change your name, language and colour at any time. You do that on **My profile**.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Home** at the bottom.
2. Tap your name at the top.
3. Tap the pencil at the bottom right of your avatar, or the **Name & colour** tile.
4. Change your name, language or colour.
5. Tap **Save**.

#### What you then see

Under **Name & colour** the fields fold open. After **Save** they fold shut and you see your new name and colour.

(`#/more/profile`)

### Dutch or English

#### What it does

The app works in Dutch and in English. You switch with one tap.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **NL** or **EN** at the top right.

#### What you then see

All buttons and texts switch to that language straight away.

#### Good to know

The app remembers the language with your profile. Switch profile and the language switches too. The same choice is in **More** under **Settings**.

### The five tabs

#### What it does

There are five tabs at the bottom. They take you everywhere in the app.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Home**, **Recipes**, **Week**, **Shopping** or **More** at the bottom.

#### What you then see

**Home** shows today and **Recipes** the whole book. **Week** is your week plan and **Shopping** your list. **More** holds your profile, the settings, **In & out** and help.

#### Good to know

Is there a number on **More**? Then something new has arrived in **In & out**.

### What Home shows

#### What it does

Home is your start screen for today. You see at a glance what to cook tonight and how your week is going.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Home** at the bottom.

#### What you then see

At the top you see today's date; tapping it opens the **Cooking calendar**. Below it are your avatar and name. Then come the **Tonight** card, the **This week** card and a few rows.

#### Good to know

Tap your name for **My profile**. Pulling Home down from the top does the same. With badges on, your level shows under your name too. If you just looked at a recipe, it says “Continue with:” and its name. That line goes away three hours after that recipe’s preparation time, or when you tap the × cross.

### The Tonight card

#### What it does

The **Tonight** card suggests one dish. If you have a week plan, it is the next dish from your week.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Home** at the bottom.
2. In the **Tonight** card, tap **Something else** for the next dish from your week.
3. Tap **Something outside your week** for a dish that is not in your week.
4. Tap **Another** for yet another dish.
5. Tap **Open** to open the recipe.

#### What you then see

The card shows the dish with its cooking time and category. Without a week plan, **Another** picks a random dish. Under **filters** you choose what **Another** may pick from.

#### Good to know

**Back to your week** takes you back to your week plan. The filters only apply to **Another**, never to your week.

(`#/`)

### The This week card

#### What it does

The **This week** card shows in short how your week is going.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Home** at the bottom.
2. Tap the **This week** title to open your week plan.
3. Tap **Shopping list** to open your list.

#### What you then see

On the right it says how many dishes are cooked, for example 2 of 7 cooked. Below are the names; what you cooked is crossed out.

#### Good to know

No week plan yet? Then it says **Pick 7 for this week**. When everything is cooked, it says **All cooked — pick new ones?**.

(`#/`)

### The rows lower down on Home

#### What it does

Below the cards are quick ways to what you often need.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Home** at the bottom.
2. Scroll down.
3. Tap a row or a tile.

#### What you then see

You see **In & out** with what is new, your **Favourites**, **Recently cooked** and a row of **Categories**. At the very bottom are **My recipes** and **The story of this book**.

#### Good to know

A dish you cooked without stars shows **Rate**. Tap it to give stars.

(`#/`)

### A new version of the app

#### What it does

Sometimes there is a new version of the app. The app never reloads by itself; you choose the moment.

#### Where you tap

1. Look at the bottom, above the tabs, for a blue bar.
2. Tap **Reload** in the bar.

#### What you then see

The bar says **New version**. After **Reload** the app loads again, with your recipes and lists still in place.

#### Good to know

The bar stays until you tap it. So you can finish cooking first.

### The test version with the other icon

#### What it does

Next to the normal app there is a test version for trying new things early. It is the test version with the other icon and its own data.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the link to the test version that Stijn sends you.
2. Install it like the normal app.
3. From now on, open it from its own icon.

#### What you then see

You have two icons; the second one says Recepten NEXT. In the test version the word NEXT shows at the top right.

#### Good to know

The test version has its own data. What you save there is not in the normal app, and the other way round. So keep your real recipes in the normal app.

### Protecting your data

#### What it does

Every time it starts, the app asks the phone to protect your data. Protected storage is not cleared lightly when the phone fills up.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **More** at the bottom.
2. Tap **Storage & backup**.
3. Look under **Status** at **Storage protected**.

#### What you then see

It says yes, no or unknown.

#### Good to know

Protected does not mean your data stays when you remove the app. A backup is the real guarantee.

(`#/more/storage`)

### The notice that you are in the browser

#### What it does

If you open the app in a browser instead of from the icon, a box at the top says so. It explains how to install the app.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the app in your phone's browser.
2. Read the box at the top.
3. Tap **Stay in the browser** if you want to carry on in the browser.

#### What you then see

The box says you are using the app in the browser, with the steps for your phone. After **Stay in the browser** the box goes and a small card shows at the bottom.

#### Good to know

The box comes back after a week. On Android in a browser other than Chrome, it comes back every time you open the page. In the installed app you never see it.

### Open in Chrome (Android)

#### What it does

A browser other than Chrome keeps everything in storage of its own. What you save there does not reach the app. **Open in Chrome** takes you to Chrome.

#### Where you tap

1. Look at the box at the top.
2. Tap **Open in Chrome**.
3. Nothing happens? Then tap **Copy the link**.
4. Open Chrome.
5. Paste the link into the address bar.

#### What you then see

If your browser cooperates, Chrome opens with the same page. After **Copy the link** a line under the buttons says the link was copied.

#### Good to know

Not every browser follows the button, and the app cannot force it. Copying and pasting into Chrome is the sure way.

## Recipes

Browsing, searching and filtering, reading and adjusting a recipe, versions for a diet, and adding recipes yourself.

### Browse all recipes

#### What it does

The Recipes list shows every recipe from A to Z, with a letter above each group. Own and received recipes carry a small label, your favourites a star.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Swipe up and down through the list.
3. Slide your finger along the letters on the right to jump to a letter.
4. Tap a recipe to open it.

#### What you then see

At the top you see how many recipes there are. While you slide along the letters, the letter shows large on the screen.

#### Good to know

The letters on the right only show with **A–Z**, without filters or a search word.

(`#/recipes`)

### Find a recipe

#### What it does

The search box finds recipes by name, ingredient, category, cuisine or label. You can type Dutch or English; accents do not matter.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Tap the search box at the top.
3. Type a word, for example onion, pasta or Italian.

#### What you then see

The results come in groups: **By name**, **With this ingredient**, **Category** and **Tag**. Each recipe shows in one group only.

#### Good to know

Search looks through every recipe that fits your filters, also when you sort by **Own**. A cuisine, such as Italian, shows under **Category**. Chosen filters sit at the front of their row, with a cross ×; after three hours the app forgets them.

(`#/recipes`)

### Choose the order of the list

#### What it does

Above the list you choose how the recipes are ordered: A to Z, what you viewed last, only your own recipes, or own and received together.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Tap **A–Z**, **Recently viewed**, **Own** or **Own & received** above the list.

#### What you then see

With **Own** you only see your own recipes, the most recently changed at the top. With **Recently viewed** the recipe you opened last is at the top.

#### Good to know

The app remembers your choice on this phone. What you viewed only counts on this phone. A tweaked classic does not count as an own recipe.

(`#/recipes`)

### Filter by diet

#### What it does

The Diet row only shows recipes that are vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free or lactose-free. With options too, recipes that offer it as an option join in.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Tap a button in the **Diet** row, for example **Vegetarian**.
3. Tap **options too** as well, if you like.
4. Tap the cross × in the bottom row of buttons to clear all filters.

#### What you then see

The list gets shorter and the top says how many of the total are left. The chosen button is coloured and has a cross ×.

#### Good to know

Choose two diets and a recipe must fit both. **options too** only works once a diet is on. The app remembers your filters on this phone for three hours.

(`#/recipes`)

### Filter by cuisine

#### What it does

The Cuisine row shows cuisines with a little flag, such as 🇮🇹 Italian. That way you only see dishes from that country.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Slide the **Cuisine** row sideways until you see the cuisine.
3. Tap a cuisine, for example 🇮🇹 Italian.

#### What you then see

Only dishes with that cuisine stay. The chosen cuisine gets a cross × and moves to the front of the row. Choose two cuisines and you see the dishes of both.

#### Good to know

The row only shows cuisines that occur in your recipes. A recipe without a cuisine drops out once a cuisine is on. Three hours after your last choice the app forgets the cuisine again.

(`#/recipes`)

### Filter by label or category

#### What it does

Below Diet and Cuisine there is a row of labels, such as Quick and Oven, and categories, such as Soup and Pasta.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Slide the bottom row of buttons sideways.
3. Tap a label or a category.
4. Tap the cross × at the start of that row to clear everything.

#### What you then see

The button turns coloured, gets a cross × and moves to the front of the row. The list gets shorter. Two categories show both; two labels must both fit.

#### Good to know

Tap a chosen button again to switch it off. Three hours after your last choice the app forgets the filters; then everything is off again.

(`#/recipes`)

### Side dishes

#### What it does

Side dishes such as mashed potatoes or garlic bread have a category of their own. A recipe page shows which dishes go with it.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Tap Side dish in the bottom row of buttons.
3. Open a side dish.
4. Scroll to **Goes with**.

#### What you then see

Under **Goes with** the matching dishes show as buttons. Tap one and that recipe opens.

#### Good to know

The Recipes list always shows side dishes too. Picking and surprising in the week skip them, unless you switch on **sides & sweet too**.

(`#/recipes`)

### View a recipe

#### What it does

The recipe page shows the name, the diet labels, the category and cuisine, the time and where the recipe comes from. Below are the buttons, the ingredients, the method and your notes.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Tap a recipe.

#### What you then see

One line with a small clock gives the time, such as “20 min · 55 min total · classic”. You also see whether it is your own recipe and how often you cooked it. Below are **Cook**, **✓ Cooked**, **Share** and **+ This week**.

#### Good to know

When a diet label says probably, the app is not sure about one ingredient. That label is then not certain. In the time, the first is how long you are busy yourself; the second is the whole time, waiting included.

(`#/recipes`)

### For more or fewer people

#### What it does

With minus and plus you change the number of people. Every amount follows.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap − or + under the heading **Ingredients**.

#### What you then see

Next to the buttons you see the new number with the recipe's own number after it. Each line shows the original amount small in brackets.

#### Good to know

The green words in the steps, **Copy as text** and **Cook** use the new number.

### Oven temperature in °C, °F or both

#### What it does

With **Temperature** you choose whether the oven temperatures in the steps show in °C, in °F or in both.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **More** at the bottom.
2. Tap **Settings**.
3. Tap **°F** or **both** next to **Temperature**.

#### What you then see

With **°F** the steps show the °F value instead of °C. With **both** the °F value follows the °C in brackets.

### Green ingredients in the steps

#### What it does

Ingredients a step mentions are green and underlined. Tap one and you see how much of it you need.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Scroll to **Method**.
3. Tap a green, underlined word.
4. Tap it once more to hide the amount.

#### What you then see

The amount appears in brackets after the word, for example (2 cloves).

#### Good to know

If you switch on **Show amounts** in cook mode, they show here straight away too.

### Make a recipe a favourite

#### What it does

The star makes a recipe a favourite. Your favourites are on Home under **Favourites**.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap the star ☆ next to the name.

#### What you then see

The star fills in (★). In the Recipes list a star shows after the recipe.

#### Good to know

Favourites belong to your profile. Without a profile you cannot switch the star on.

### Add a photo to a recipe

#### What it does

You can add your own photos to a recipe. The newest one shows large at the top of the recipe.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap **+ Photo** under the time line.
3. Choose a photo or take one.

#### What you then see

The photo shows large at the top and small in a strip, with a circle in your colour.

#### Good to know

Photos do not travel when you share a recipe. They only go along in a backup with photos.

### Delete a photo

#### What it does

A photo you no longer want is removed from the large view.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe with a photo.
2. Tap the small photo in the strip.
3. Tap **Delete** in the large view.
4. Tap **Yes, delete**.

#### What you then see

The photo is gone, also at the top of the recipe.

#### Good to know

This cannot be undone.

### Your own note on a recipe

#### What it does

Under **Notes** you write something for yourself, such as less salt. Only you see it and it is never translated.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Scroll to **Notes**.
3. Type your note in the box.

#### What you then see

After a moment Saved appears. You do not need to tap anything.

#### Good to know

A note belongs to your profile and does not travel when you share. To pass something on, use **Maker's tip** in the editor.

### Copy a recipe as text

#### What it does

**Copy as text** puts the recipe on your clipboard as plain text. It is in your language and for your number of people.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap **Copy as text**.
3. Tap **Whole recipe** or **Ingredients only**.
4. Paste the text wherever you like, for example in WhatsApp.

#### What you then see

You briefly see Copied ✓. The text has the name, the ingredients with dashes and numbered steps.

### Print a recipe (Android)

#### What it does

**Print** makes a black-and-white print version of the recipe, without buttons or photos.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap **Print**.
3. Tap **Whole recipe** or **Ingredients only**.

#### What you then see

Your phone's print preview opens.

#### Good to know

On the iPhone there is no Print button. Instead you see the line ‘To print on iPhone: Share → Print.’

### Versions for a diet (gluten-free, vegan …)

#### What it does

Above the ingredients there is sometimes a row of buttons: Original, Gluten-free, Vegetarian, Vegan and Lactose-free. The app suggests swaps itself, such as gluten-free pasta.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap a button above **Ingredients**, for example **Gluten-free**.
3. Swipe left across the ingredients to move to the next button.

#### What you then see

A button with a dashed outline is still a suggestion. Swapped lines have a coloured bar; an orange dot means: check yourself.

#### Good to know

If a recipe is already vegetarian, that button has a ✓ and cannot be chosen. The row only appears when there is something to choose.

### Approve a version

#### What it does

You approve a suggestion once. After that it is a fixed version of this recipe.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap a button with a dashed outline, for example **Gluten-free**.
3. Tap **Check and approve**.
4. In the window, untick what you do not want swapped.
5. Tick the box **Checked** on every line with a !.
6. Tap **Approve** at the top of the window.

#### What you then see

The button is now filled in and you briefly see, for example, ‘Gluten-free version approved’. Above the ingredients it says who approved it and when.

#### Good to know

This does not make a new recipe. **Approve** only works once every line with ! is ticked. If the recipe changes later, the app asks you to **Check again**; **Withdraw** turns it back into a suggestion.

### The chosen version comes along

#### What it does

The app remembers which button you chose for each recipe. An approved version goes along to cook mode, your week and the shopping list.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap an approved version, for example **Gluten-free**.
3. Tap **Cook** or **+ This week**.

#### What you then see

Cook mode shows the swapped lines, with the name of the version above the step. In your week a button with the version shows under the dish.

#### Good to know

A version that is still a suggestion does not go along: you then cook the original. The chosen button is remembered on this phone.

### Your own version of a recipe

#### What it does

With **Make my own version** you make your own variant of a recipe. The original stays as it is.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Scroll all the way down.
3. Tap **Make my own version**.
4. Change what you like in the editor.
5. Tap **Save**.

#### What you then see

Your version is called, for example, ‘Goulash (Stijn's version)’ and has a button back to the original. The original gets a button with your name, with **Open as a recipe** inside.

#### Good to know

You need a profile. You can share your own version on its own.

### Maker's tip

In the box **Maker's tip** you write something for whoever gets your recipe. The tip travels with the recipe when you share it.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap **Edit this recipe** at the bottom.
3. Type your tip in the box **Maker's tip**.
4. Tap **Save**.

#### What you then see

Below the method there is a card with 💡, ‘Tip from’ and your name, with your text.

#### Good to know

Your private note never travels. Under **Share** you can send your note along once as the tip.

### Add a recipe (the +)

#### What it does

The + at the top right adds a recipe. You choose from four ways.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Tap the plus sign + at the top right.
3. Tap one of the four choices.

#### What you then see

A window slides up with **Paste a recipe**, **Write it yourself**, **With AI** and **Choose a file**, each with one line of explanation.

#### Good to know

Home has the same + at the top right. **Close** or swiping down closes the window without doing anything.

### Write a recipe yourself

#### What it does

In the editor you type the name, the number of people, the ingredients and the steps yourself.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Tap the plus sign + at the top right.
3. Tap **Write it yourself**.
4. Type the name in the box **Name**.
5. Type the first ingredient under **Ingredients**.
6. Tap **+ line** for the next ingredient.
7. Type the first step under **Method**.
8. Tap **Save** at the top.

#### What you then see

Chips appear under each ingredient: green with ✓ when the app knows it, orange with ? when it does not. After **Save** your new recipe opens.

#### Good to know

You need at least a name, one ingredient and one step. A line ending in a colon becomes a heading, such as Dressing:.

(`#/add`)

### Paste the whole method at once

#### What it does

Do you have the steps as one block of text? Paste it in one go; the app turns it into separate steps.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the editor of a new or own recipe.
2. Tap **Paste whole method** under the steps.
3. Paste the text into the box.
4. Tap **Split into steps**.

#### What you then see

The steps now stand separately under **Method**. Blank lines become step boundaries; long paragraphs are split.

### The language of a recipe in the editor

#### What it does

At the top of the editor you choose whether you write in Dutch, in English or in both.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the editor of a recipe.
2. Tap **NL**, **EN** or **both** at the top.

#### What you then see

With **both**, the Dutch and the English stand side by side. Recognised ingredients show the dictionary translation in grey in the other column.

### Adjust an ingredient line with the chips

#### What it does

Under a recognised ingredient there are chips with the amount, the unit and the name. Tap them to adjust them.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the editor of a recipe.
2. Tap a green chip under an ingredient.
3. Change **Quantity**, **Unit** or **Preparation**.
4. Tap **Apply**.

#### What you then see

The text of the line follows your choice.

#### Good to know

**Optional** marks something you can leave out. **Other ingredient** links the line to something else.

### Which word is the ingredient?

#### What it does

When the app does not know a line, an orange chip with a question mark shows under it. With it you point out which word is the ingredient.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the editor of a recipe.
2. Tap the orange chip with the question mark.
3. Tap the words that are not the ingredient, so they turn grey.
4. Tap **Link like this** at the top of the window.

#### What you then see

The window **Which word is the ingredient?** shows the words as buttons: green is the ingredient, grey becomes a note. After **Link like this** the chips are green.

#### Good to know

The text you typed does not change; only the link does. If the app finds nothing, tap **New ingredient** or **Search instead**.

### Variant or new ingredient?

#### What it does

When the app knows part of the name, as with tasty Parmesan, it asks whether it is a variant or something new.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the editor of a recipe.
2. Tap **Link** under a line with a ?.
3. Tap the button that starts with Variant of, or **New ingredient** or **Search for something else**.

#### What you then see

The window asks: ‘Is this a variant of …, or a completely new ingredient?’ With a variant your text stays and the extra word becomes a note.

#### Good to know

If the app knows nothing of the name, the search box opens straight away.

### Choose category, cuisine and labels

#### What it does

The last card of the editor is called **Labels**. There you choose the category, the cuisine and the diet labels.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the editor of a recipe.
2. Scroll to the card **Labels** above **Save**.
3. Choose a category under **Category**.
4. Tap a cuisine under **Cuisine**.
5. Tap the labels that fit under **Diet & labels**.

#### What you then see

For a new recipe the app fills in a suggestion based on the ingredients. It follows your edits until you choose yourself.

#### Good to know

The app never suggests a cuisine itself. If yours is missing, pick it under **Other cuisine…**. Probably on a label means: not certain, tap to confirm.

### Quickly change the category

#### What it does

On the recipe page you change the category without opening the editor.

#### Where you tap

1. Open a recipe.
2. Tap **change** next to the category.
3. Choose another category from the list.

#### What you then see

The new category shows under the name straight away.

#### Good to know

On a classic the app keeps this as your tweak; the original stays.

### Paste a recipe from a website

#### What it does

Paste the text of a recipe website or a message. The app finds the name, the servings, the ingredients and the steps itself.

#### Where you tap

1. Copy the whole page on the website.
2. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom of the app.
3. Tap the plus sign + at the top right.
4. Tap **Paste a recipe**.
5. Tap **Paste from clipboard**.
6. Tap **Fill in**.

#### What you then see

Under the box you first see, for example, ‘Recipe from a website: 8 ingredients, 6 steps’. After **Fill in** everything is in the form.

#### Good to know

iPhone: if **Paste from clipboard** does not work, press and hold the text box and tap Paste. If pasting does not work well, use **With AI**.

### The Check box after pasting

#### What it does

Lines the app could not place after pasting are in the box **Check**. There you put them in place or remove them.

#### Where you tap

1. Paste a recipe from a website.
2. Find the box **Check** above the form.
3. Tap **→ ingredient**, **→ step** or **remove** on a line.

## Sharing & receiving

You send a recipe, your week plan, your card or your progress to someone else in a few taps. Here you read how to share and how to receive, on Android and on the iPhone.

### What you can share

#### What it does

You can share a recipe, your week plan, your shopping list, your card and your progress. Several recipes at once go in one message, or in a few messages if there are many.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the recipe, week plan, list or profile you want to share.
2. Tap the share button on that screen, for example **Share** or **Share week plan**.
3. Choose WhatsApp or another app.

#### What you then see

Almost everywhere three buttons sit side by side: share, copy and **Show QR**. What you share is a short message with one link.

#### Good to know

The receiver needs the app to keep anything. For people without the app, a recipe has **Share as text**.

### Share a recipe

#### What it does

You send one recipe as a WhatsApp message with a link. The other person keeps it in their own app with one tap.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Recipes** at the bottom.
2. Tap the recipe you want to share.
3. Tap **Share**.
4. Tap **Share via WhatsApp**.
5. Choose WhatsApp and the person you are sending it to.

#### What you then see

First you see the message as it will be sent, with the link at the bottom. Then your phone's share menu opens.

#### Good to know

Have you adjusted a classic? Then only your adjustment travels, without a duplicate recipe for the other person. If you have a note of your own on the recipe, a switch lets you send it along as the maker's tip.

(`#/recipes`)

### Let someone scan a QR code

#### What it does

Next to every share button there is **Show QR**. The other person scans the code with their phone camera and gets the same link as in the message.

#### Where you tap

1. Open the screen where you share, for example **Share** for a recipe.
2. Tap **Show QR**.
3. Let the other person scan the code with their phone's camera app.
4. Tap **Show QR** again to put the code away.

#### What you then see

Under the buttons a QR code appears on a white square, also in the dark theme. Under it: “Let the other person scan this with their phone camera.”

#### Good to know

If the link is longer than 1,600 characters, you see no code but “Too big for a QR code. Share the link instead.” That happens with about half of the recipes. On an iPhone the scanned code opens Safari, never the app itself.

### Share a recipe as plain text

#### What it does

For people without the app you share the whole recipe as readable text. The link comes last, for anyone who does have the app.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap a recipe.
2. Tap **Share**.
3. Scroll down to **Share as text**.
4. Choose the language of the text under **Language**, if that choice is there.
5. Tap the **Share as text** button.

#### What you then see

You see the recipe as the other person will read it: name, ingredients and steps. **Copy text** puts it on your clipboard.

### Too much for one message: several messages

#### What it does

Many recipes do not fit in one WhatsApp message. The app then spreads them over a few messages. Each message works on its own.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **More** at the bottom.
2. Tap **Recipes & sharing**.
3. Tap **Share your new recipes**.
4. Tap a name under **To whom?**.
5. Tap **Share via WhatsApp**.
6. Send the first message in WhatsApp.
7. Tap the button “Share part 2 of 2”.
8. Send this message in WhatsApp too.

#### What you then see

Above the button it says “This goes in 2 messages.” Each message starts with a heading like “🍲 3 recipes from Gabi (part 1 of 2)”. After each message it says, for example, “Part 1 of 2 sent”.

#### Good to know

Every recipe is in exactly one message. The receiver pastes the messages one by one in **In & out**; on Android they tap the link in each message. **Copy** copies all the messages at once. A file only comes with a very long recipe or with **Export**; then tap **Also send the explanation** after sharing.

(`#/share`)

### Share several recipes at once

#### What it does

You pick a few recipes from the list and share them in one go. If they do not fit in one message, they go in a few messages.

## Dictionary

The dictionary knows the ingredients, their names in two languages, their aisle and their unit. Here you read how to look things up and add to it.

### What the dictionary is

#### What it does

The dictionary knows more than 1500 ingredients, each with a Dutch and English name, an aisle and a default unit. With it the app translates ingredient lines, units too, such as tbsp and el.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **More** at the bottom.
2. Tap **Dictionary** under **Recipes & sharing**.

#### What you then see

At the top there is a search box, a counter of built-in and own ingredients, and a row of aisles. Below that are your own ingredients.

#### Good to know

Built-in ingredients can be looked at but not changed. Own ingredients you can edit, merge and delete.

(`#/more/dictionary`)

### Look up an ingredient

#### What it does

Search for an ingredient by its Dutch or English name, or look at everything in one aisle.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Dictionary** in **More**.
2. Tap the search box.
3. Type a few letters, in Dutch or English.
4. Tap an aisle in the row under the search box, if you like.
5. Scroll to the end of the list.
6. Tap **Show more** for the next ones.

#### What you then see

The top says how many ingredients were found. With an aisle chosen you only see that aisle, from A to Z.

#### Good to know

**All** shows every aisle again. You can search and use an aisle at the same time.

(`#/more/dictionary`)

### What is known about an ingredient

#### What it does

Tap an ingredient and you see everything the app knows about it.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Dictionary** in **More**.
2. Search for an ingredient.
3. Tap the name.

#### What you then see

A box opens with the Dutch and English name, the plural, other names, the aisle and the unit. Small labels show properties such as vegetarian or contains lactose.

#### Good to know

You cannot change anything in this box. Tap the name once more to close it.

(`#/more/dictionary`)

### Add an ingredient yourself

#### What it does

If an ingredient is missing, you create it yourself. It gets the small label own.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Dictionary** in **More**.
2. Tap **+ New ingredient** at the top.
3. Type the Dutch name.

## Badges, XP and levels

Cooking earns XP, XP takes you up a level, and badges and levels unlock fun extras. Everything here belongs to the **Badges** switch: switch it off and none of it shows.

### How you earn XP

#### What it does

You earn XP by cooking, with a photo of your dish, with a recipe of your own and with badges. Browsing, searching, sharing, planning and shopping earn no XP.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap a recipe.
2. Tap **✓ Cooked**.
3. Tap **Save** at the top.
4. Look at the bottom, just above the tabs.

#### What you then see

A strip slides in at the bottom with your level, your XP bar and, on the right, for example “+105 XP”. The bar fills up and the number counts along. Then the strip slides away again.

#### Good to know

A badge or a completed challenge brings that strip too. Tap it to close it straight away. A photo or a recipe of your own brings no strip, but a small chip at the top right, such as “+30”. The first time you cook a recipe gives 100 XP; each next time 10 less, but never less than 50. A recipe with many ingredients gives up to 30 extra, stars give 5 extra. A first photo of a dish gives 30, a recipe of your own 10, or 15 when it is in two languages.

### Extra XP: days in a row, a full week and badges

#### What it does

Cook more days in a row and cooking gives more XP. A week in which you cook every day gives 50 extra XP, and every badge gives XP too.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **Home** at the bottom.
2. Tap your avatar or your name at the top.
3. Look at the flame under the XP bar.

#### What you then see

Under the bar it says “🔥 … days in a row”, or “🔥 cooked today” on your first day. If you skipped a day, “free day used” follows.

#### Good to know

From 3 days in a row cooking gives 10 % extra, rising to double after a year. Skipping one day a week is allowed. A badge gives 50 XP per tier; a secret badge 250. The week runs from Monday to Sunday.

(`#/more/profile`)

### Why you sometimes get 0 XP

#### What it does

Sometimes cooking earns no XP, so the game stays fair. The app simply tells you so.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap a recipe.
2. Tap **✓ Cooked**.
3. Under **When?**, tap **Another date**.
4. Choose a day more than a week ago.
5. Read the line under the date.

#### What you then see

You read “More than a week ago: counts for your badges and the calendar, not for XP.” After the sixth cook on one day you see “That's plenty for today — XP again tomorrow.”

#### Good to know

The same recipe counts once a day. The fourth and fifth cook on a day give half, from the sixth nothing. If you cook four or more times a day for four days in a row, you see “Busy kitchen week! XP again tomorrow.” XP is never taken away.

### The blue, gold and red chips

#### What it does

After a photo or a recipe of your own, a chip with the XP appears briefly at the top right. Sometimes it is extra much: that is luck, not a mistake.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap a recipe.
2. Under **Photos**, tap **+ Photo**.
3. Choose a photo of your dish.
4. Look at the top right of the screen.

#### What you then see

Most of the time the chip is blue. A gold chip with “Cooked!” means double XP; a red chip with “OVERCOOKED!” five times as much.

#### Good to know

Every reward varies a little, up to a fifth more or less. With “OVERCOOKED!” the screen shakes briefly, unless **Confetti** is switched off. When you cook, that luck shows in the big word instead of a chip: see “Cooked! big on the screen”.

### Cooked! big on the screen

#### Where you tap

#### What you then see

In the middle of the screen “Cooked!” appears in gold, among the confetti. With double XP the word glows extra. With five times the XP it turns into a red “Overcooked!”, the screen shakes and the word stays a little longer.

#### Good to know

One tap during the celebration makes the rest twice as fast. The word belongs to the confetti: with **Confetti** off, no word appears. If you cook with another typeface from **Your style**, the word uses that typeface. If your phone is set to reduce motion, the word stands still.

#### Good to know

XP is per profile: everyone on this phone has their own level. The first levels go quickly, after that each level takes longer. On Home your level sits under the greeting.

## iPhone and Android

It is one app, but an iPhone and an Android phone cannot do everything the same way. Here is honestly what differs.

### Receive a recipe

#### What it does

On Android the app appears in your phone's share menu. On the iPhone that is not possible: there you copy the message and paste it into the app.

#### Where you tap

1. Android: press and hold the message in WhatsApp.
2. Android: tap the share icon.
3. Android: choose Recepten.
4. iPhone: press and hold the message in WhatsApp.
5. iPhone: tap Copy.
6. iPhone: open the app from the icon on your Home Screen.
7. iPhone: on **Home**, tap the **In & out** row.
8. iPhone: tap **Paste from clipboard**.

#### What you then see

On both phones **In & out** opens with a preview of the recipe. Tap **Import** to keep it.

#### Good to know

On the iPhone you can also press and hold in the big text box and choose Paste. That route always stays in the app.

(`#/inbox`)

### Does a link open the app?

#### What it does

On Android a share link can open the app, if your phone is set up for it. On an iPhone a link always opens Safari, never the app.

#### Where you tap

1. Android: tap the share link in WhatsApp.
2. iPhone: tap the share link in WhatsApp.
3. iPhone: tap **Copy recipe code** in Safari.
4. iPhone: open the app from the icon on your Home Screen.
5. iPhone: tap **More** at the bottom.
6. iPhone: tap **In & out**.
7. iPhone: tap **Paste from clipboard**.

#### What you then see

On Android the app opens, or Chrome. On the iPhone Safari shows a preview of the recipe; Safari itself saves nothing.

#### Good to know

Does Chrome open on Android after all? No harm done: Chrome and the app share the same storage, so the recipe reaches the app too. Android decides; the app cannot force it.

(`#/inbox`)

### The Opening links in the app window

#### What it does

**More** has a window with the steps for each phone. The phone you are holding comes first.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **More** at the bottom.
2. Tap the **Help & about** heading.
3. Tap **Opening links in the app**.
4. Tap **Close** when you are done.

#### What you then see

A window with an Android part and an iPhone part, each with numbered steps. On Android in another browser it also shows **Open in Chrome**.

#### Good to know

On Android you make Chrome the default browser there and switch Open supported links on. On the iPhone it explains the copy-and-paste route.

(`#/more`)

### Timers with the screen off

#### What it does

A timer surely runs while the app is open and the screen is on. With the screen off or the app in the background, that is not certain.

#### Where you tap

1. Start a timer in a recipe.
2. Keep the app open until the time is up.

#### What you then see

When the time is up, you hear the timer sound. In cook mode the screen stays on by itself.

#### Good to know

On Android a short timer also went off with the app in the background; a long one has not been tried yet. On the iPhone this has not been tried yet. For a long time, also set an alarm on your phone.

### Vibration

#### What it does

On Android the phone vibrates when a timer is done. On an iPhone the app itself does not vibrate.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **More** at the bottom.
2. Tap the **Settings** heading.
3. Look under **Cook mode & timer** at **Vibrate**.

#### What you then see

You see the **Vibrate** switch, with no explanation under it. On an iPhone only the notification vibrates.

#### Good to know

Whether that notification arrives on the iPhone while the app is in the background has not been tried yet.

(`#/more`)

### Voice commands and reading aloud

#### What it does

Reading aloud works on both phones. Voice commands, such as next or timer 10 minutes, only work on Android.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **More** at the bottom.
2. Tap the **Settings** heading.
3. Look under **Cook mode & timer**.

#### What you then see

On Android you see the switches **Read aloud** and **Voice commands**. On an iPhone, instead of the second, it says voice commands only work on Android.

#### Good to know

Voice commands usually need internet.

(`#/more`)

### Save a file

#### What it does

You can share or download a backup or recipes file. Where a download ends up differs per phone.

#### Where you tap

1. Tap **More** at the bottom.
2. Tap **Storage & backup**.
3. Tap **Save or share**.
4. Tap **Download**.
5. iPhone: tap Download when the phone asks.

#### What you then see

On Android the file is in the Downloads folder. On the iPhone it is in Files → Downloads.

#### Good to know

With **Share** you choose where it goes, for example WhatsApp to yourself, Drive or Files. That works on both phones.

(`#/more/storage`)

### Receive a file

#### What it does

Did you get a recipes file in WhatsApp? On Android you share it straight to the app. On the iPhone you save it in Files first.

#### Where you tap

1. Android: press and hold the file in WhatsApp.
2. Android: tap the share icon.
3. Android: choose Recepten.
4. iPhone: save the file from WhatsApp to Files.
5. iPhone: open the app from the icon on your Home Screen.
6. iPhone: on **Home**, tap the **In & out** row.
7. iPhone: tap **Choose file**.
8. iPhone: pick the file in Files.

#### What you then see

**In & out** shows the recipes from the file, each with what is new. Tap **Import** to keep them.

#### Good to know

**Choose file** works on Android too.

(`#/inbox`)

### Scan a QR code

#### What it does

The app makes QR codes but does not scan them itself. You always scan with your phone's camera app.

#### Where you tap

1. Open your phone's camera app.
2. Point the camera at the QR code.
3. Tap the link that appears.

#### What you then see

On Android the app or Chrome opens, just like with a share link. On the iPhone Safari opens with a preview and **Copy recipe code**.

#### Good to know

On the iPhone you then copy the code and paste it into the app through **In & out**.

### Installing

#### What it does

On Android you install the app through Chrome. On the iPhone you add it to your Home Screen through Safari.

#### Where you tap

1. Android: open the link in Chrome.
2. Android: tap the ⋮ menu at the top right.
3. Android: tap **Install app**.
4. iPhone: open the link in Safari.
5. iPhone: tap **Share** at the bottom.
6. iPhone: choose **Add to Home Screen**.

#### What you then see

On Android the icon is in your app drawer. On the iPhone it is on your Home Screen.

#### Good to know

On the iPhone the Home Screen app has storage of its own, separate from Safari. What you save in Safari is not in the app. On Android Chrome and the app share their storage.

### Updating

#### What it does

Updating works the same on both phones. The app never loads a new version by itself; you tap.

#### Where you tap

1. Look at the bottom, above the tabs, for a blue bar.
2. Tap **Reload**.

#### What you then see

The bar says **New version**. After **Reload** the app loads again.

#### Good to know

You never need to update anything through an app store.

### The Open in Chrome notice

#### What it does

On Android in a browser other than Chrome, the app shows the **Open in Chrome** button. On the iPhone that button never shows.

#### Where you tap

1. Android: look at the box at the top.
2. Android: tap **Open in Chrome**.
3. iPhone: look at the box at the top.

#### What you then see

On Android there are two buttons: **Open in Chrome** and **Copy the link**. On the iPhone the box tells you to add the app to your Home Screen.

#### Good to know

Another browser, such as Opera or Samsung Internet, has storage of its own. What you save there does not reach the app.
